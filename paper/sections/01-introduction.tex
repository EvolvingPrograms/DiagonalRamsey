\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

We reproduce the weighted-potential method of Zhao, Booth-Clibborn and
Schwahn~\cite{ZBS} and obtain a small improvement to their diagonal
Ramsey bound. The principal contribution is the reproduction of their
method; the refinement lowers the exponential base from $3.4986238$
to $3.49135655$.

Let $R(k,\ell)$ be the least $N$ such that every red--blue colouring of
$K_N$ contains a red $K_k$ or a blue $K_\ell$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For all sufficiently large integers $k$,
\[
 R(k,k)\le 3.49135655^k.
\]
\leannote{\texttt{eventual\_diagonal\_bound},
\leanfile{S8_RamseyBound/L4_MainTheorem.lean\#L36}{L4_MainTheorem.lean}}
\end{theorem}

The classical bound $R(k,k)<4^k$ is due to Erd\H{o}s and
Szekeres~\cite{ES}. Campos, Griffiths, Morris and Sahasrabudhe first
reduced the exponential base~\cite{CGMS}; subsequent developments include
the optimization of Gupta, Ndiaye, Norin and Wei~\cite{GNNW}, the
retained-set descent of Lu and Wang~\cite{LW}, and the weighted-potential
construction of Zhao--Booth-Clibborn--Schwahn~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{ZBS}.

Our refinement retains an additional consequence of maximality. A
blue-neighbourhood restriction and its full complement satisfy two
moment inequalities. Retaining the complementary inequality excludes
some retained fractions, while preserving the previous optimizer at
every surviving fraction. We certify a negative integral envelope for
this smaller feasible set and retune the source/profile construction.

We reuse the potential, concentration, ordered drift, finite-host closure,
and source/profile framework of~\cite[Sections~3--5]{ZBS}, together with
their supporting data and the uniform source bound of
Lu--Wang~\cite[Lemma~B.1]{LW}. A retuned construction yields a dense
diagonal exponent $z=625145177/500000000$. Colour symmetry and the strict
inequality $e^{z+10^{-9}}<3.49135655$ give Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
The finite certificate data and corresponding Lean proofs are in the
\href{https://github.com/EvolvingPrograms/DiagonalRamsey}{accompanying repository}.
We do not give a numerical starting threshold for $k$.
